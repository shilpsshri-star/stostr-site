The problem

  • Eight finished listings in two markets (UK and US) and no one seeing them.
  • Listings that weren't written for how buyers search.
  • No social presence pointing people to the shop, and no way to tell what would work.

What we set up

  • A listing audit: titles and all 13 search tags rewritten around real buyer searches.
  • A two-a-day post plan, one for the UK and one for the US, built from the real product designs, with a tracked link on every post.
  • A tracker with a public, read-only dashboard and a written experiment log.

What we're learning

  • Search, not social, is where ready-to-buy shoppers arrive, so listing quality is the first lever.
  • The selling season is short (October to January), which makes testing quickly worth more than polishing.
  • Every week the log records what was tried, what changed, and the decision taken.

How it works For the technically curious
  • The owner logs a few numbers each morning (views, visits, orders, posts, link clicks, listings optimised) in a Google Sheet. A Dashboard tab of formulas turns them into totals, a daily trend and an experiments table.
  • That tab is published read-only; this page fetches it in the visitor's browser, draws 14-day bars and the funnel, and calculates each experiment's before and after from the daily rows.
  • Every Monday a scheduled Claude task reads the tracker, writes the weekly read, and proposes one experiment for the owner to approve. Posts are drafted by AI and approved by a person before they're scheduled.
